Why “no distortion” is a choice

A sphere cannot be flattened without stretching something. Carl Friedrich Gauss proved it, and Tissot showed the consequence: a flat map can hold area true or shape true, never both at once. These equal-area projections keep every country’s share of the surface exact and spend the error on shape, mostly near the poles and the outer edges.

Zooming, honestly

Pan and zoom scale the whole plate uniformly, so nothing shifts relative to anything else: every figure in the size check holds at any magnification. Coastlines are drawn from 1:50 m data, detailed enough to hold up several steps in — far enough that you can put Greenland and the Congo side by side and see the answer yourself.

What Mercator is for

Mercator is not a bad map, it is a navigator’s map: a straight line on it is a constant compass bearing, which is why it rules at sea and on screens that pan and zoom. The price is area. At 70°N it magnifies by roughly nine, which is how Greenland ends up the size of Africa — Africa is about fourteen times larger.
